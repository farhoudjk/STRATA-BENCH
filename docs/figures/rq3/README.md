# RQ3 · N-gram speculative decoding: does it pay off, and on which engine?

Generated by `scripts/plot_rq3.py` (PDF). Mistral-NeMo-12B, L40S, client concurrency 256, five
arrival processes; no speculation vs n-gram speculation with k = 3 draft tokens; max_num_seqs 32
and 64 (kept low because SGLang caps running requests at 48 under speculation); workloads
`mixed_real` (TTFT from arrival) and `chat_multiturn` (TTFT from send). TensorRT-LLM requires its
overlap scheduler to be disabled for n-gram speculation, so its speculative runs differ from its
baseline in two ways. Numbers are in the CSVs next to each figure.

## Flow
1. **F1 payoff matrix:** what speculation changes on every metric, per engine (headline).
2. **F2 by task type:** *where* it pays: tasks whose outputs repeat the prompt.
3. **F3 by load:** *when* it pays: light load; the gain shrinks or reverses as batches fill.
4. **F4 measurement trap:** why per-chunk latency reports the opposite of the truth.
5. Supplementary: **S1** SLO goodput, **S2** TTFT under load.

## F1 · `fig_rq3_1_payoff_matrix.pdf`
**Caption.** Change with n-gram speculation relative to no speculation, per workload,
max_num_seqs and engine: TTFT p50, TPOT p50/p95, longest token gap p95, E2E p50, output
throughput, and strict goodput (percentage points). Purple = better, red = worse.

**Insight.**
- **vLLM benefits consistently:** E2E p50 −12 to −28%, throughput +14 to +70%, TPOT p50 −8 to
  −12%, and far fewer stalls on multi-turn chat (longest gap p95 −58 to −64%); goodput +1 to
  +5 pp.
- **SGLang gains throughput but not tail latency:** throughput +58 to +63% on `mixed_real` and
  E2E p50 −2 to −21%, but TPOT p95 rises 14–24%; goodput unchanged (±1 pp).
- **TensorRT-LLM loses:** median TTFT on `mixed_real` rises 2.6× (+162 to +166%), TPOT p95 +26
  to +58%, E2E p50 +7 to +20%, goodput −5 to −14 pp. Part of this is confounded by the disabled
  overlap scheduler.
- **The same feature has opposite effects on different engines**; a study run on one engine
  would not predict another.

## F2 · `fig_rq3_2_by_task.pdf`
**Caption.** Change in median TPOT with n-gram speculation by task type (`mixed_real`,
max_num_seqs 64), with median output length.

**Insight.** N-gram drafting reuses text already in the context, so it pays where outputs repeat
the prompt or themselves: **reasoning** (vLLM −24%, SGLang −22%, TensorRT-LLM −10%) and **code**
(SGLang −34%, TensorRT-LLM −24%). On free-form **chat** and on **summarisation** drafts are
rarely accepted, and verification is pure overhead: SGLang +13% and +23%, TensorRT-LLM +23% on
chat. vLLM never gets slower on any task (−1% to −24%). vLLM's acceptance-rate metric is
server-wide (≈0.35 for every task), so it cannot attribute acceptance to tasks.

## F3 · `fig_rq3_3_by_load.pdf`
**Caption.** Change in median TPOT with n-gram speculation vs arrival rate; max_num_seqs 64
(solid) and 32 (dashed).

**Insight.** Speculation trades idle compute for fewer decoding steps, so it pays when batches are
small and stops paying when they fill. On `mixed_real`, SGLang's gain of −21% at 2 req/s becomes
a loss of +8% from 8 req/s, and vLLM's shrinks from −12% to −2%. On `chat_multiturn`, vLLM gains
10–14% at every rate, SGLang turns from neutral to +7–11% slower, and TensorRT-LLM is 24–33%
slower at every rate with max_num_seqs 64 (8–10% with 32).

## F4 · `fig_rq3_4_measurement_trap.pdf`
**Caption.** Left: change with n-gram speculation in the per-request median gap between streamed
chunks (ITL p50, light) and in time per output token (TPOT p50, solid). Right: median tokens per
streamed chunk.

**Insight.** With speculation, accepted tokens arrive several per chunk (SGLang 1.50,
TensorRT-LLM 1.54, vLLM 1.17 tokens per chunk). The gap between chunks therefore grows (+57%
SGLang, +47% TensorRT-LLM, +9% vLLM) even though each token arrives sooner (TPOT −12%, −3%, −9%).
A benchmark that reports ITL concludes the opposite of the truth. Report TPOT.

## S1 · `fig_rq3_s1_goodput.pdf` (supplementary)
**Caption.** Strict and relaxed SLO goodput with and without speculation.

**Insight.** Speculation does not change who meets the SLO except on TensorRT-LLM, which loses
9–14 pp on `mixed_real` through slower first tokens; vLLM gains up to 6 pp on `chat_multiturn`.
Under these SLOs TTFT, not per-token speed, decides goodput (as in RQ1), which caps what any
decode-side optimisation can buy.

## S2 · `fig_rq3_s2_ttft_load.pdf` (supplementary)
**Caption.** TTFT p95 vs arrival rate with and without speculation (`mixed_real`, max_num_seqs 64).

**Insight.** vLLM and SGLang keep their first-token latency; TensorRT-LLM's rises at every rate
(2 req/s: ≈9 → 20 s), consistent with losing the overlap scheduler, which hides CPU scheduling
work behind GPU execution.

---

## Recommendation (for the decision table)
| Engine | Use n-gram speculation? |
|---|---|
| vLLM | yes, on any workload; largest gains on reasoning and code at light-to-moderate load |
| SGLang | only for reasoning/code-heavy traffic at light load; not for chat or under heavy load |
| TensorRT-LLM | no, in this configuration (overlap scheduler must be off) |

## Caveats
- One model and one draft length (k = 3); learned drafters (EAGLE, Medusa) behave differently.
- TensorRT-LLM: a baseline with the overlap scheduler disabled is needed to separate the cost of
  speculation from the cost of losing overlap scheduling.
- SGLang caps running requests at 48 under speculation, so max_num_seqs 64 runs it with fewer
  concurrent requests than its baseline.
- Acceptance rate is observable only on vLLM, and only server-wide.
